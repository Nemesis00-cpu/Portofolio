TOPIC 1

The Present Continuous Tense

The Present Continuous Tense describes an activity that is happening now or around the present time. Look at these examples: I am reading a book. She is washing her hands. They are cooking dinner. We are studying English.

All of these activities are happening at the time of speaking.

The Formula

Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing. Every Present Continuous sentence follows it.

A. Affirmative sentences

Structure
Subject + am / is / are + verb-ing
SubjectBeExample
IamI am studying.
He, She, ItisShe is cooking.
You, We, TheyareThey are playing.

More examples: I am cleaning my bedroom. Kevin is fixing his bicycle. My parents are visiting my grandparents. The children are playing in the garden.

Short forms

In everyday English we often use contractions: I'm, He's, She's, It's, We're, You're, They're. For example: I'm doing my homework. She's washing the dishes. They're watching television.

B. Negative sentences

We put not after am, is, or are. The verb ending in -ing does not change.

Structure
Subject + am / is / are + not + verb-ing

Examples: I am not sleeping. He is not playing football. We are not having lunch. · Common contractions: is not → isn't and are not → aren't, as in It isn't raining. They aren't having a picnic.

C. Interrogative sentences

To make a question, place am, is, or are before the subject.

Two Question Patterns
Am / Is / Are + subject + verb-ing? Question word + am / is / are + subject + verb-ing?

Yes/No questions: Is Sarah reading a book? Are you doing your homework? · With question words: What are you doing? Where is she going? Why are they laughing? Who is cooking dinner?

D. Short answers

POSITIVE

Yes, I am.

Yes, he is. / Yes, she is.

Yes, they are.

NEGATIVE

No, I'm not.

No, he isn't. / No, she isn't.

No, they aren't.

IN USE

Is your brother fixing his motorbike? — Yes, he is.

Are your cousins having fun? — No, they aren't.

E. Forming verbs with -ing

Most verbs simply add -ing: read → reading, wash → washing, cook → cooking, visit → visiting, listen → listening, play → playing. But three groups of verbs change their spelling first.

RULE 1 · SILENT E

Remove the final e, then add -ing.

make → making, ride → riding, write → writing, bake → baking, dance → dancing

RULE 2 · SHORT CVC

Double the final consonant, then add -ing.

run → running, swim → swimming, sit → sitting, get → getting, begin → beginning

RULE 3 · ENDING IN IE

Change ie to y, then add -ing.

lie → lying, die → dying, tie → tying

F. The four uses of the Present Continuous

1 · RIGHT NOW

An action happening at the moment of speaking.

Mother is cooking dinner right now. It is raining outside.

Time words: now, right now, at the moment, currently

2 · TEMPORARY

An activity happening for a limited period — not permanent.

My cousin is staying with us this week. Father is working from home this month.

3 · AROUND NOW

In progress during the current period, but maybe not this exact second.

My brother is studying abroad. They are preparing for the school festival.

4 · FIXED FUTURE

A future plan that is already arranged.

We are visiting Grandmother tomorrow. They are having a party tonight.

A future time expression makes the meaning clear.

Example in Daily Life

It is seven o'clock in the evening. Rina and her family are at home. Her mother is cooking in the kitchen. Her father is reading a newspaper in the living room. Rina is doing her homework in her bedroom. Her younger brother is playing with his toys. Their cat is sleeping under the table.

All five activities are happening at the same time — that is exactly what the Present Continuous is for.

TOPIC 2

House and Home Vocabulary

A house has different rooms, furniture, and parts. Learning these words helps us describe where we live and talk about activities at home.

Tip

Learn each word together with a sentence — Mother is cooking in the kitchen teaches vocabulary and grammar at once.

Interactive 3 · Explore
Walk through the house

Click each room to see what is inside it. Notice that every example sentence uses the Present Continuous — that is how the two halves of this unit fit together.

B. Furniture and household objects

WordMeaningExample
ArmchairA comfortable chair for one person, usually with arm supportsGrandfather is sitting in the armchair.
CarpetA thick material placed on the floorThe children are sitting on the carpet.
CurtainA piece of cloth that covers a windowPlease open the curtain. The room is dark.
CupboardUsed for storing plates, cups, glasses, or foodThe glasses are in the cupboard.
FridgeKeeps food and drinks coldWe keep milk, cheese, and vegetables in the fridge.

C. Parts outside or around a house

GATE

Opens and closes at the entrance to a house, garden, or yard.

Father is opening the gate.

ROOF

The top part of a house. It protects the house from sun and rain.

The roof is wet because it is raining.

GARDEN

An area where people grow flowers, grass, fruit, or vegetables.

The children are playing in the garden.

BACKYARD

An open area behind a house.

My brother is riding his bicycle in the backyard.

Example in Daily Life

Dina lives in a small but comfortable house. There is a gate in front of the house and a red roof on top of it. The living room has a sofa, an armchair, a carpet, and two curtains. The kitchen has a fridge and a cupboard. There are two bedrooms and one bathroom. Dina usually puts her shoes in the hall before entering the living room.

One short paragraph can describe the rooms, the furniture, and the parts of a house all at once.

TOPIC 3

Listening to Conversations

In daily life we listen to classmates, teachers, family members, announcements, videos, and telephone conversations. In this unit you will practise listening to short conversations about social events — a party and a picnic.

The tasks ask you to identify feelings, time, relationships, addresses, music preferences, people, and food. Below are the five strategies you need.

Golden Rule

Read the questions before you listen. It tells you what to listen for.

A · MAIN IDEA

What is the conversation generally about?

Words like birthday, music, Saturday, friends, address together suggest an invitation to a party.

B · SPECIFIC INFO

Names, times, dates, addresses, numbers, relationships, food, opinions.

Listen carefully — several numbers or names may be mentioned, and speakers often correct themselves.

C · FEELINGS & OPINIONS

Feeling words: happy, excited, worried, tired, bored, nervous, surprised.

"I do not know anyone there." → the speaker feels worried.

Opinion phrases: I think… I like… It sounds fun… I prefer…

D · RELATIONSHIPS

sister, brother, cousin, friend, classmate, neighbour.

"Who is Rachel?" — "She is my friend from school."

E · MATCHING

Match people with food, drinks, or objects.

Strategy: read all names → read all choices → listen for one person at a time → write a temporary answer → check on the second listening.

The Present Continuous is used for activities happening right now, temporary situations, activities around the present period, and fixed future arrangements.
